Where the engine stalls

Eight fuel lines leak at the same three joints.

Strategy, creative, media, PR, content: every line feeds one engine. Most stalls trace back to one of these joints.

JOINT 01

Intake without a filter

Briefs arrive in Slack threads, forwarded emails, and hallway asks. Without one intake with real QA, every fuel line enters the engine dirty.

JOINT 02

Launches without a gate

Campaigns go live because the date arrived, not because a readiness check did. One missing owner, one untracked asset, and the launch leaks.

JOINT 03

Measurement in four dialects

Strategy reads one dashboard, creative another, the client a third. One engine needs one measurement standard, or nobody agrees it ran.

The ops layer

What keeps it one engine.

Not more retainers. Not more meetings. Four pieces of boring, reliable project operations.

LAYER 01

Intake QA

One front door for every request: owner, deadline, assets, success metric. Nothing starts until all four exist.

What changes: no more briefs that begin with "can you just quickly..."

LAYER 02

Launch gates

A short readiness checklist before anything goes live. Green means go, red means hold. No hallway launches.

What changes: launches become readiness-driven, not date-driven.

LAYER 03

One measurement standard

Same definitions and same dashboard for strategy, creative, activation, and the client. One engine, one set of gauges.

What changes: "did it work" stops being a debate.

LAYER 04

Learnings loop

Every launch ends with a short memo: what worked, what stalled, what changes next. The engine tunes itself instead of repeating stalls.

What changes: the engine improves every quarter on purpose.

What I would own as your project coordinator.

Concrete moves in my real stack, sequenced for the fastest visible effect.

Stand up one intake form

Owner, deadline, assets, and success metric, all required, in ClickUp. An n8n automation routes each request to the right pod and flags incomplete briefs.
